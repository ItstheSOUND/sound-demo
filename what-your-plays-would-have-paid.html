The artist calculator

What your plays would have paid

Pull up your dashboard. Spotify for Artists, Apple Music for Artists, Bandcamp, whichever one you check. It shows you two numbers for the month: how many times your songs were played and how many people played them. Put them here and see what the same plays would have paid on SOUND, with the arithmetic printed underneath so you can check it.

SOUND does the arithmetic the way you'd assume it already worked. A subscription is $11.99. The first 15.3 percent comes off the top and goes to songwriters and their publishing organizations, a legally set pass-through we keep none of. Out of what's left we pay the bills, every one of them itemized in public. Whatever remains is the artist pool, and nobody takes a margin out of it. Divide that pool by all the plays that earned it and what falls out is the per-play rate. It is not a number anyone chooses. It is arithmetic: pool on top, plays on the bottom.

Before you send this to anyone, the caveats

Launch is smaller than the illustrative month. At launch, with a small subscriber base, the per-stream rate will be low, possibly lower than what you earn now. But it's real, it's published every month and it rises with every single subscriber, because there's no management cut taken before it exists and no label position diluting it.

SOUND does not pay a flat rate per play. There is no global number every stream is worth. A listener's $11.99 goes only to the artists they played, split by how much they played each of them, so the figure above assumes your listeners listen an average amount overall. If your fans play you and not much else, each fan is worth more than this implies; if you're one song in a 400-play month, less. The 2 cents is the thermometer. The user-centric split is the body.

SOUND has no free tier, so every play here is a paid one. And all of it is illustrative and pre-launch: the artist agreement is not a signed document, the company is being formed and SOUND has not paid a cent to anyone. This is what the rate does to your plays, not a check anyone is writing.
